Option A

Phase strip

One slim card above the day cards: phase + block, this block's focus in a sentence, and the next check-in date glowing brass. Everything deeper (the full target table, gate chains) lives behind the "Targets & progressions" row on its own page.

  • Home stays gym-fast — day cards still dominate; zero scroll cost.
  • The check-in date is always in your face, which is the thing that actually triggers a pivot.
  • Focus line doubles as the block's "why" — cheap to update per block.
  • Long-term goals are a tap away, not visible — less daily gravity from the marks themselves.

Option B

Targets board

The full scoreboard lives on the homepage, below the day cards. Every mark: current → target with a luminance progress bar. The muscle-up renders as its true shape — a gate chain, current gate lit brass. Deadlift sits visibly dimmed with a "later phase" tag, so the long arc is honest without pretending it's active.

  • Maximum gravity: you see the distance to every mark, every time you open the app.
  • Gate-chain pips are the right grammar for progressions (muscle-up now; dips/DL phase-gates later).
  • Bars are luminance-only (brass on graphite) — reads without hue.
  • Homepage gets tall; day cards compete with a wall of numbers before a session.
  • Needs current-×5 estimates per lift — app tracks working weight, so "current" is honest, but bench (DB→barbell) needs a judgement call.

Option C

The ladder

A vertical timeline under the day cards: where you are (brass dot), the next check-in, the phase gates ahead with their entry conditions, the target numbers, and maintenance mode as the explicit endpoint. It's the only option where when to pivot is structural — every future step names its trigger condition, not just a date.

  • Answers "what's the plan?" in one glance — phases, gates, and the finish line exist in sequence.
  • Pivot conditions written on the rungs ("squat ≥200×5 → DL enters") make check-ins decisive instead of vibes.
  • Cheap to maintain: it's words + dates, no live computation needed to ship v1.
  • No per-lift progress bars — less "how close am I" fidelity than B.
  • Longest card of the three; likely wants a collapsed one-rung view with "show the road" expand.

Hybrids are cheap: A's strip as the always-visible header with B's board or C's ladder behind the tap is the likely end state — strip answers "what now / when next," the deeper page answers "how far / what road."
