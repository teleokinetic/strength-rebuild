Pressed botanical — variations

Four riffs on the pressed-oak lane. Every phone keeps the rest of the package — tucked-corner boxes, the filling rest pill — so you're only judging the specimen. Each drawing is shown big on a plate, then in place. Any of them can sit in the home bottom corner and the workout header corner interchangeably.

Variation A

Oak, redrawn

The same twig with a better hand: a true lobed oak leaf with a midrib, a smaller one answering it lower down, and plumper acorns with solid caps. Still line art, still quiet.

Variation B

The rooted oak

Not a twig — the whole tree, as a solid bookplate mark, roots breaking through the ground line. Grown, not assembled, which is rather the point of a rebuild. It stands in the home corner like a stamp; on workout it would shrink to an emblem beside the day name.

Variation C

Laurel

The classical strength emblem: a laurel sprig with paired leaves and three berries at the tip, filled rather than outlined. The most graphic and even-textured of the four — it reads cleanly even smaller.

Variation D

Barley

A pressed field specimen instead of a forest one: a bearded barley head on a tall stem, awns fanning up, two thin blades low. Harvest and patience — the quietest and most vertical, it hugs the right edge without spreading.

All four sit at the same brass, same opacity, same placements — home's open bottom corner and the workout header corner — so they're interchangeable once you pick. The rooted oak also works as a pair: tree on home, its twig (Plate A) on workout. Same species, two scales.
