Forte — a design pass in four moments

The skin is right; what's missing is feel. The app doesn't know where Carolina is mid-workout, ends a session with a toast, and keeps her progress invisible. Four moments fix that — every one is additive UI on the existing engine, no new logging burden.

Decisions locked: "Up next" is cut, relative dates wait for a first completed session, the Progresso row is the badge cut, and the ladder tops out at a first full set of push-ups. The four small fixes shipped as v1.2.1 and a full click-through verification passed clean. (Portugal note: the greetings are identical in European Portuguese, and the date line already localizes to her iPhone's language.)

Already sings, don't touch: safe-area handling, wake lock during rest, the silent draining-pill timer, sticky rest dock, petal corners, the pressed sprig, and the one-weight-per-lift model.

Moment 1 · Home

The home feels awake

Today the two cards are identical twins and the screen goes quiet below them. Three small moves make opening the app feel like being expected:

  • Greeting follows the clock — Bom dia / Boa tarde / Boa noite. Three lines of code, and the app feels awake. (The date line already localizes to her iPhone's language on its own.)
  • A glyph per day, drawn in the sprig's hand: Terra gets a sprouting seed, Voo a swallow. The days finally look like their names.
  • Relative dates, earned — "Fri, Aug 1 — 3 days ago" appears only once that day has a completed session. Before that the card says "First one's waiting" (already live in v1.2.1).
  • One Progresso row under the cards — the only new navigation on the whole pass, and the home's empty bottom half earns its keep. The badge cut, as picked: a solid rose disc gives the row one strong anchor the day cards don't have.

Moment 2 · Mid-workout

A sense of place, no logging tax

The day screen looks identical at minute 0 and minute 50 — the biggest single thing keeping it from feeling alive. One tap fixes it:

  • A ring on each card. Tap it when the exercise is done: the card settles back (dims), a petal fills on the trail under the title. Ephemeral state — it lives in the active session, never gets logged, and Finish works fine if she ignores rings entirely.
  • The rest dock gets smart for free. Each exercise already declares its rest tier in the data (rest: 'heavy') — the UI just never used it. With rings, the app knows the current lift, so the tier it wants takes the rose and says why.
  • Menu bullets become rung pills. Tap the variant you did today. It reads as "where am I on the ladder," takes the same space, and quietly feeds the Progresso ladder in Moment 4.
  • Done cards collapse to one line — the list gets shorter as she works, which is the feeling of progress.
  • One extra tap per exercise. Optional by design, but worth watching whether she actually uses it.

Moment 3 · Finish

Finishing earns a bloom

Right now the finish screen is a sentence, a textarea, and a button — and the reward for 48 minutes of work is a toast. This merges confirm + recap + celebration into one closing card:

  • The recap shows exactly what will save. Today that promise is abstract ("saves every tracked lift at the weight shown on its chip"); here she can read it — and a wrong prefill jumps out before it's in the log.
  • The sprig blooms here and only here. The bloom-when-done idea lost as a persistent ornament — as a once-per-session moment it's exactly right. Petals open one by one (skipped under reduced motion).
  • Duration and count make the session feel like a finished object, not a form submission.
  • Rung picks and menu notes land in the recap's last line, so "menu" exercises finally show up at the finish too.
  • Praise sits above a not-yet-tapped Save — deliberate: the workout is the achievement, saving is bookkeeping. Save stays one tap.

Moment 4 · Progresso

Progress she can see

The view you've been holding off on — here's the shape I'd give it. Not a targets ledger: three ways of seeing that it's working, hero first.

  • The push-up ladder is the hero. It's the goal she named, so it goes on top: rungs from the seed program's own menu, current rung marked, the first full set of push-ups waiting at the top with a bloom. Fed by the rung pills from Moment 2 — zero extra input.
  • Trends are one sparkline and one sentence. Working weight over time, computed from the sessions log that already exists. No goals, no grades, no red.
  • Chin-up celebrates the falling number. Assistance trending down reads as loss unless the copy flips it — "less help = stronger."
  • Rhythm over records. At two sessions a week, consistency is the real stat. Week dots say "you keep showing up," which is the thing worth saying.
  • Needs a few logged sessions before it's interesting — the empty state should invite, not apologize: "two more sessions and the lines appear."
Build: medium — all computed from existing data

Small fixes, no wireframe needed

  1. Empty chips read as broken. A fresh install shows "— lb ×— ▾" on every card. Until a weight exists, the chip should invite: "add weight". First-run is the one session where the app has to sell itself.
  2. "‹ Back" is a small target. Give it real padding (44 pt). It's tapped with chalky thumbs between sets.
  3. First-run day cards say "Not yet logged." Bookkeeping voice. Say "First one's waiting" — an invitation instead of an empty ledger. Same slot, same size, warmer app.
  4. Note placeholder "What happened?" reads like an incident report. "How did it go?" matches the app's voice. One string.

Left out on purpose: the feedback floater — you asked for an options rundown before anything gets built, so it's not wireframed here. And nothing above touches the engine: rings and rung picks live in the active session, Progresso is computed from the sessions array, and the seed program stays untouched.
