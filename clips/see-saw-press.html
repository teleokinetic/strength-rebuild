      Before you start

      • Shoulder check. Stand with your back, head and hips against a wall and raise both straight arms overhead. If your thumbs can't reach the wall without your low back lifting off it, start with a regression (below).
      • Pain. Stop and tell your coach if you feel pinching or pain in the shoulder, neck, elbow or wrist. Don't push through it.
      • Breathing. Breathe out as each bell goes up; don't hold your breath. If you have high blood pressure or heart problems, or are pregnant, keep the breath moving and check with your provider about overhead lifting.
      • Starting weight (coaching estimate, not research). Use two bells you can press about 8 clean reps each side, one arm at a time. The see-saw is harder than it looks.
      • If a bell stalls, bring it back down to your chest. Don't lean back to grind it up.
      • Regressions. One-arm press → half-kneeling one-arm press (makes leaning back harder) → landmine press (less overhead range).

      Coach's cue card

      Setup

      • Two equal bells, lighter than your one-arm press weight.
      • Get both bells to the rack: a double clean, or clean one at a time. Check both racks before the first press.
      • Feet hip-width, weight over the middle of the foot, knees straight but not locked.
      • Handle low in the palm, wrists straight, elbows tucked onto the ribs. Squeeze your glutes and brace your abs.
      • Press one bell up from the double rack, then start the see-saw.

      Cues

      1. "Press one up as you lower the other." (Some coaches say "pull it down" to keep the lowering bell smooth.)
      2. "Pass at head height."
      3. "Arm by your ear, ribs down."

      Common faults → Fix

      • Leaning back, ribs flaring → Fix: breathe out on the press, squeeze your glutes, go lighter.
      • Top bell dropping into the rack → Fix: slow down (try 0.5×) and count the down and the up together.
      • Bell finishing in front of the face → Fix: finish with the arm by your ear and the bell over the middle of your foot; if you can't get there, use a regression.
      • Leaning away from the pressing arm → Fix: lighter bells; keep the ribs over the hips and press a little forward of the ear.
      • Wrist bent back → Fix: handle low in the palm, knuckles toward the ceiling.
      • Elbow flaring straight out to the side → Fix: let the elbow travel forward and out, about halfway between straight ahead and out to the side.

      Not a fault: pressing one bell, bringing it back to the rack, then pressing the other is the regular alternating press. It's a different exercise, not a mistake.

      Tempo

      Handover~2 sPause~1 sHandover~2 s

      Each handover about 2 seconds, same speed up and down · short pause at the top, about 1 second · no rest in the rack.

      Finish

      Bring the top bell down to the rack. Then lower both bells to your hips and down to the floor with a flat back: hinge at the hips, don't round.

      About the evidence. Standing one-arm presses made the trunk muscles work harder than seated or two-arm presses (Saeterbakken & Fimland, 2012), and standing presses gave the most shoulder-muscle activity (Saeterbakken & Fimland, 2013). No study has tested the see-saw itself; its benefits here come from coaching experience. The effort arrows and the figure are illustrations, not measurements.
